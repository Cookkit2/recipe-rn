## TL;DR

Cookkit is already **more feature-rich than nearly every direct competitor** — including Samsung Food, which is still paying to catch up on features Cookkit already ships (camera ingredient scanning, pantry-aware grocery dedup, in-store grocery mapping, voice-guided cooking, YouTube import, food-waste tracking, shared households, gamification).

The highest-leverage improvements are **not new features**. They are **monetization rigor** and **Day-0 onboarding** — both backed by RevenueCat's 2025/2026 benchmarks across 115k+ apps. The single most important fact: **~55% of trial cancellations and ~50% of paid conversions happen on Day 0**, so the first session is where the product is won or lost.

This roadmap (**v2 — solo/indie build, revenue & conversion focus**) sequences **23 initiatives** across 4 dimensions (Monetization, AI, UX, Performance) into **Now / Next / Later / Deferred** execution tracks, grounded in verified competitive research and a direct inventory of Cookkit's actual code. All 23 are live on GitHub (#717–#739).

## How this roadmap was built (methodology)

1. **Deep competitive research** — a 106-agent workflow that fetched 24 sources, extracted 113 claims, and **adversarially verified 25** with 3 independent votes each (16 confirmed, 9 killed). 11 findings survived synthesis. ChefGPT and several SuperCook marketing claims were correctly **refuted** during verification and are excluded.
2. **Codebase grounding** — a direct read of Cookkit's routes, models, hooks, and utils to confirm what already exists, so the plan targets real gaps instead of duplicating working features. Two research "open questions" were resolved in code (see below).
3. **Issue drafting** — a 44-agent draft→adversarial-refine pipeline wrote detailed, file-referenced issue bodies and checked every file reference, citation, and dependency for accuracy.

> **Evidence caveat:** The dedicated recipe-manager segment (Paprika, Mela, Crouton, Pestle) and meal-planner segment (Mealime, Eat This Much, Prepear) did **not** yield confirmed 3-vote claims in research — their killer features are an evidence gap (addressed by issue **P4-3**). RevenueCat figures are **category-agnostic medians to A/B test, not guaranteed lifts** for Cookkit.

## Two code facts that reshaped the plan

- **Cookkit's grocery list already does pantry-aware dedup — and better than Samsung Food.** `hooks/queries/useGroceryList.ts` builds a pantry index keyed by normalized names *and* `IngredientSynonym` synonyms, converts units, subtracts on-hand stock, and marks each item `isCovered` / `neededQuantity` / `pantryQuantity`. Samsung Food's documented weakness ("adds everything including items you already have") is **already solved here**. → This becomes a **near-zero-code hero differentiator for ASO + onboarding** (P1-3), not a build item.
- **Pro-gating is fragmented across 3 independent mechanisms** (`useFeatureFlag` that defaults false while loading, an imperative `isValidSubscription()` call, and a hand-rolled `useQuery` in `SubscriptionCard`). → Centralizing it (P0-1) is the prerequisite for every monetization experiment.

## Verified research findings (the evidence base)

- **[F1]** SuperCook wins with one-tap **voice-dictation batch pantry entry** ("open fridge, tap mic, speak"). Copyable; Cookkit has the speech-recognition pieces already. *(App Store)*
- **[F2]** Samsung Food **adds everything to the grocery list including items you already have** (staples heuristic only). Cookkit already outperforms this. *(Google Play + Samsung support, unfixed thru v5.50)*
- **[F3]** Samsung Food+ launched **photo ingredient scanning (Vision AI) at ~$7/mo** (IFA 2024), partly appliance-tied. Confirms Cookkit's camera scanning is competitively relevant. *(Samsung press, The Verge 2024-08-31)*
- **[F4]** **Photo-recognition — not the recipe LLM — is the accuracy bottleneck** (SnapChef, n=21). Isolate/validate the recognition layer; add confidence + correction + fallback. *(CEUR ICAIW 2024)*
- **[F5]** **Hard paywalls convert ~5× freemium** (10.7% vs 2.1% D35) but with higher refunds (5.8% vs 3.4%) and ~same Y1 retention (27–28%). A conversion lever, not a retention strategy. *(RevenueCat 2025/2026)*
- **[F6]** **17–32-day trials convert ~70% better than ≤4-day trials** (42.5% vs 25.5%), counter to the industry's shift to short trials. *(RevenueCat)*
- **[F7]** **Day 0 is decisive:** ~55% of trial cancels + ~50% of paid conversions happen on Day 0; 80–90%+ of trials start on install day. First-session "aha" (<60 min) is the top target. *(RevenueCat)*
- **[F8]** **Pricing dominates retention:** ~30% of annuals cancel in month 1; cheap annual retains up to **36% Y1 vs 6.7%** for high-priced monthly. *(RevenueCat 2025)*
- **[F9]** **AI apps monetize harder but churn faster:** +41% Y1 RLTV, +52% trial conversion, but ~30% faster churn and +20% refunds. Retention is the defensive priority as Gemini features deepen. *(RevenueCat 2026)*
- **[F10]** **CozZo shut down late 2025** when its cloud was decommissioned — validates **offline-first local-DB as a moat**. *(cozzo.app)*
- **[F11]** **Pure-CRDT conflict resolution is costly**; Figma/Linear use **hybrid local-first UX + server-authoritative sync**. Don't over-invest in pure-CRDT. *(Local-First: Promises & Pitfalls, 2025)*

## Gap analysis by dimension

### 🎯 Monetization & Growth — the largest levers (best-cited)
Gating is fragmented and the funnel is unmeasured. RevenueCat data points to hard paywalls (5×) [#F5], long trials (+70%) [#F6], cheap-annual default (36% vs 6.7% Y1) [#F8], and an AI-churn risk to engineer against [#F9]. **Start by centralizing entitlement (P0-1) and instrumenting the Day-0 funnel (P0-2)**, then run the paywall/trial experiments. **Strategic wrinkle from segment research [#738 ✅]:** the recipe-manager leaders (Paprika/Mela/Pestle) are deliberately pay-once and market "no subscription" as a moat — Cookkit must counter-position its subscription (justify via AI features they lack) and/or add a Lifetime tier (**#745**).

### 🤖 AI & Smart Features
Import, tailored-recipe, and a 9-strategy recommender are real — but **nothing auto-generates a meal plan (P2-1)**, **photo-recognition is the unhardened bottleneck (P2-2)**, **voice input is cooking-only (P1-2 reuses it for pantry)**, and **on-device AI is an empty scaffold (P4-1)**.

### 🧭 Core UX & Onboarding
**Day-0 "aha" is the #1 priority (P1-1)** [#F7]. Home is pantry-only — test a discovery/plan-forward IA (P3-1). Cooking-screen polish (P2-3) and accessibility (P3-2) are parity gaps.

### ⚙️ Performance & Offline
The WatermelonDB+Supabase hybrid is the pragmatic choice [#F11]; offline-first is a moat [#F10]. Harden cold-start/TTI (P3-3), image cache + offline write-queue (P3-4), and household multi-device conflict UX (P0-3 audit → P3-5).

## P0-1 — Centralize subscription entitlement into a single useEntitlement/isPro hook

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dimension** | Monetization |
| **Phase** | 0 — Foundations |
| **Priority** | P0 |
| **Type** | infra |
| **Estimate** | M |
| **Depends on** | _none_ |

> **Summary:** Pro-gating is fragmented across three independent mechanisms (a remote useFeatureFlag that defaults to false while loading, an imperatively-called isValidSubscription in one screen, and a hand-rolled useQuery in SubscriptionCard) that each re-fetch entitlement state independently. Introduce a single useEntitlement()/isPro hook backed by the existing entitlements query key so gating is consistent, instant offline, and testable across every Pro surface.

### Motivation
Today there is no single source of truth for "is this user Pro", and each consumer reaches entitlement state differently:

1. hooks/queries/useFeatureFlags.ts — useFeatureFlag(key) fetches ONE remote flag at a time from data/supabase-api/ReviewApi.fetchFeatureFlag and returns `enabled: data?.enabled ?? false`. Because it defaults to enabled:false while isLoading, any gate wired to a flag renders as "locked" on first paint and on every cache miss — a real usability bug for the three surfaces currently using it for `ratings_and_reviews` (app/(misc)/search.tsx, app/recipes/[recipeId]/index.tsx, components/Recipe/Step/CongratulationsContent.tsx). A feature flag is also the wrong abstraction for entitlement: an entitlement is a local RevenueCat fact, not a remote experiment toggle.
2. app/profile/create-household.tsx — calls isValidSubscription().then(...) imperatively inside a useEffect and stores the boolean in useState, completely bypassing the TanStack cache. A navigation pop/push re-runs the async check from scratch.
3. components/Profile/SubscriptionCard.tsx — hand-rolls its own useQuery keyed to subscriptionQueryKeys.entitlements() that calls isValidSubscription() (which calls Purchases.getCustomerInfo()) on every mount/cache miss, duplicating the work that utils/subscription-utils.ts presentPaywallIfNeeded() ALSO does with a second getCustomerInfo() call.

Meanwhile the entitlement IS a React-queryable resource: app/_layout.tsx configures RevenueCat (deferred via requestIdleCallback) and registers a customerInfoUpdateListener that calls invalidateSubscriptionEntitlementsQuery() — so a single hook on subscriptionQueryKeys.entitlements() already auto-updates on purchase/restore/expiry with zero extra wiring. Nothing reads it except SubscriptionCard.

This fragmentation is the highest-leverage Monetization blocker because every later Pro experiment (hard-vs-freemium gating per [F5], longer trials per [F6], Day-0 "aha" gating per [F7]) needs a single, instant, offline-correct isPro to gate against. Per [F7], Day 0 is decisive — ~50% of paid conversions happen on install day and the first session's "aha" is the single highest-leverage target — so a gate that flickers "locked -> unlocked" because it defaults to false during a network round-trip is a plausible conversion leak (DIRECTIONAL: the RevenueCat figures are category-agnostic medians to A/B test, not guaranteed lifts for Cookkit). Per [F9], AI-powered apps monetize harder (+52% trial conversion) but churn faster and refund more (+20% refunds); as Gemini features deepen, an inconsistent gate that shows locked UI to a paying Pro user is a refund-generator. This issue is foundational and intentionally does NOT change pricing, trial length, or what is gated — it only establishes the single read path so those experiments can run cleanly against it.

### Acceptance criteria
- [ ] A single hook (e.g. useEntitlement) exists in hooks/queries/ and is the ONLY public read path for Pro entitlement state across the app; grep for `entitlements.active`, `isValidSubscription(` used as a read, `useFeatureFlag(` for entitlement purposes, and hand-rolled useQuery on subscriptionQueryKeys.entitlements() returns zero non-test call sites outside the new hook and utils/subscription-utils.ts action helpers.
- [ ] useEntitlement returns at minimum { isPro: boolean; entitlement: EntitlementInfo | null; isLoading: boolean }, where isPro is true only when an active Pro entitlement exists (the active["Pro"] entry returned by Purchases.getCustomerInfo).
- [ ] isPro is TRUE synchronously from the TanStack cache when an entitlement is cached (no network round-trip on cached reads) — the hook never renders a known-Pro user as locked on a cache hit.
- [ ] useEntitlement is keyed on subscriptionQueryKeys.entitlements() and therefore auto-updates on purchase/restore/expiry via the existing customerInfoUpdateListener in app/_layout.tsx, with no new listener registration.
- [ ] During the initial loading window (before first cache fill), useEntitlement exposes isLoading and a documented safe default so consumers can show a non-committal loading state rather than a hard 'locked' UI.
- [ ] app/profile/create-household.tsx no longer maintains its own isPro useState + useEffect; it reads isPro from useEntitlement.
- [ ] components/Profile/SubscriptionCard.tsx reads entitlement/expiry from useEntitlement instead of its hand-rolled useQuery, and still renders correct trial-vs-subscription and days-remaining copy.
- [ ] utils/subscription-utils.ts is refactored so the entitlement READ (used by the hook) is separated from the paywall PRESENT action (presentPaywallIfNeeded); presentPaywallIfNeeded continues to call invalidateSubscriptionEntitlementsQuery() on every terminal result so the hook refreshes.
- [ ] No regression in existing paywall flows: CameraActionRow, BottomActionBar, ingredient/(create)/confirmation.tsx, and SubscriptionCard still gate/present correctly (no double getCustomerInfo round-trips on cached reads).
- [ ] Unit + integration tests cover: cached hit returns isPro=true without fetching; loading state is exposed; isPro flips after a simulated purchase via the customerInfoListener invalidation; graceful behavior when RevenueCat is not yet configured (pre-requestIdleCallback) or getCustomerInfo throws.

### Technical approach
Follow the existing TanStack Query + query-key pattern already established in this repo (hooks/queries/recipeQueryKeys.ts, subscriptionQueryKeys.ts, useRecipeQueries.ts):

1. subscriptionQueryKeys.ts already exposes `entitlements()`. Keep it; this is the single key.

2. Create the hook in hooks/queries/ (e.g. useEntitlement.ts). It calls useQuery({ queryKey: subscriptionQueryKeys.entitlements(), queryFn: readEntitlement, staleTime, gcTime }) where readEntitlement wraps the read-only half of today's isValidSubscription from utils/subscription-utils.ts (Purchases.getCustomerInfo() -> customerInfo.entitlements.active["Pro"]). Note isValidSubscription already returns the entitlement object (truthy/falsy), so queryFn can return it directly and isPro is `!!entitlement`. Return { isPro: !!entitlement, entitlement: entitlement ?? null, isLoading }. Do NOT default isPro to false silently while loading (that is the current useFeatureFlag bug we are removing); expose isLoading so consumers choose. Mirror staleTime (5 * 60 * 1000) used by SubscriptionCard so cache behavior is unchanged.

3. Refactor utils/subscription-utils.ts: split isValidSubscription into (a) a pure read used by the hook's queryFn, and (b) keep presentPaywallIfNeeded as the ACTION helper. Do not change its PAYWALL_RESULT handling or the ENTITLEMENT_IDENTIFIER = "Pro" constant. presentPaywallIfNeeded already calls invalidateSubscriptionEntitlementsQuery() on NOT_PRESENTED/PURCHASED/RESTORED — that is the bridge that makes the hook auto-update; preserve it.

4. Migrate the three consumers:
   - app/profile/create-household.tsx: delete the `useState<boolean|null>` + `useEffect(() => isValidSubscription().then(...))` block; replace with `const { isPro } = useEntitlement();`.
   - components/Profile/SubscriptionCard.tsx: delete its local useQuery block; use `const { entitlement, isLoading } = useEntitlement();` and derive expiry/days-remaining from entitlement.expirationDateMillis as before.
   - Decide per-surface whether the three `useFeatureFlag("ratings_and_reviews")` call sites (search.tsx, recipes/[recipeId]/index.tsx, CongratulationsContent.tsx) should move to useEntitlement (entitlement-gated) or stay as remote feature flags (experiment-gated). Default: if the gate is "is this a Pro feature", move it to useEntitlement; if it is a genuine kill-switch/experiment, leave it on useFeatureFlag but file that decision inline. The scope of THIS issue is the entitlement hook; the flag-vs-entitlement reclassification is done as part of migration but documented, not designed from scratch.

5. Do NOT add a new Purchases.addCustomerInfoUpdateListener — the one in app/_layout.tsx already calls invalidateSubscriptionEntitlementsQuery(), which is what refreshes the hook's cache. Reuse it.

6. Preserve offline-first semantics: Purchases.getCustomerInfo() returns cached RevenueCat state offline, so on a cache miss the hook will attempt one read and then serve stale — consistent with the app's WatermelonDB+Supabase offline-first posture ([F10] validates offline-first as a moat against cloud-dependent competitors). Do not introduce a new network dependency.

7. Keep the file-by-file diff minimal and follow the repo's TDD expectation (CLAUDE.md): write the hook test first in a co-located __tests__ dir. Note there is no pre-existing hooks/queries test suite to mirror, so establish the layout with this hook.

### Test plan
- Unit (hooks/queries/__tests__/useEntitlement.test.ts): mock Purchases.getCustomerInfo to return an active Pro entitlement -> isPro===true and isLoading===false on a fresh query; mock empty active entitlements -> isPro===false; assert queryKey === subscriptionQueryKeys.entitlements(); assert that invalidating that key re-runs queryFn.
- Unit: assert that when getCustomerInfo rejects, the hook degrades gracefully (isPro===false, no uncaught throw) and the cache is not poisoned.
- Unit: assert isLoading is true on the very first render before queryFn resolves, and that a consumer can distinguish loading from 'definitely not Pro'.
- Integration: simulate a purchase by firing invalidateSubscriptionEntitlementsQuery() (the same call the customerInfoUpdateListener in app/_layout.tsx makes) and assert the hook re-renders isPro true without remounting the component.
- Integration: assert pre-configure window — if Purchases is not yet configured (the requestIdleCallback in _layout.tsx has not run), useEntitlement does not crash and reports a safe state.
- Regression manual: on a Pro account, open (a) profile/create-household, (b) the SubscriptionCard on profile, (c) a recipe detail BottomActionBar, (d) CameraActionRow — confirm no surface flashes 'locked' on a cache hit and no double getCustomerInfo network calls appear (verify via Purchases.setLogLevel DEBUG locally).
- Regression manual: complete a purchase via presentPaywallIfNeeded in any gated flow and confirm SubscriptionCard + create-household update to Pro without a full app reload.
- Grep gate (CI-sharp): `grep -rn 'entitlements.active\|isValidSubscription(' --include='*.ts' --include='*.tsx' app components hooks store | grep -v __tests__ | grep -v utils/subscription-utils.ts` returns only the new hook's queryFn.
- bun run typecheck && bun run lint pass.

### Risks
- Default-during-load regression: if the hook silently defaults isPro to false during the initial load (the exact bug useFeatureFlag has today), Pro users will see locked UI on first paint — a conversion/refund hazard per [F7] (Day-0 decisive) and [F9] (AI-app refund sensitivity). DIRECTIONAL: RevenueCat medians are category-agnostic, so the magnitude is a hypothesis. Mitigation: expose isLoading and require consumers to render a non-committal state; never return isPro=false while isLoading=true unless explicitly opted in.
- Over-scoping the flag-vs-entitlement reclassification: ratings_and_reviews may be a legitimate remote kill-switch, not an entitlement gate. Force-migrating it to useEntitlement could remove the ability to remotely disable the feature. Mitigation: migrate only entitlement-shaped gates; leave experiment/kill-switch flags on useFeatureFlag and document each decision.
- Dual-read during transition: until all call sites migrate, both the new hook and the old isValidSubscription paths exist and each calls getCustomerInfo — temporarily more network calls, not fewer. Mitigation: land the hook + consumer migrations in the same PR; do not ship the hook standalone.
- TanStack cache staleness masking expiry: a 5-minute staleTime means a Pro subscription that expires mid-session may still gate as Pro for up to 5 minutes. The customerInfoUpdateListener mitigates this for RevenueCat-pushed updates but not for all clock-bound edges. Mitigation: acceptable for a foundations issue; revisit if expiry-bound gating is added later.
- No new monetization experiment in this issue — per [F5]/[F8] pricing and trial-length changes are the real conversion levers (testable hypotheses, not prescriptions); this hook only unblocks them. Stakeholders should not expect conversion movement from this issue alone.

## P0-3 — Audit & document the sync/conflict-resolution strategy (HouseholdSyncService + WatermelonDB/Supabase)

| | |

> **Summary:** Two household-sync code paths (batch HouseholdSyncService + realtime HouseholdRealtimeService) implement an undocumented, inconsistent last-writer-wins conflict strategy, with the realtime delete path calling destroyPermanently() (no soft-delete columns on the stock table) and the batch pull path skipping timestamp comparison entirely. This issue produces an ADR-class audit documenting the actual edge-case behavior (concurrent edits, deletes, rejoins, clock skew, per-item O(rows) fetches, error swallow) so P3-5 hardening work targets real, verified gaps instead of assumptions.

### Motivation
Multi-device household sync is a Pro-tier, retention-critical surface, yet its conflict strategy is unconfirmed and, on inspection, internally inconsistent. This is dangerous to leave undocumented before P3-5 hardening because (a) the code's actual behavior contradicts what a reader would assume, and (b) the offline-first local-DB moat ([F10] — CozZo's cloud decommission / EOL build in late 2025 validates WatermelonDB-first) is only credible if sync correctness is understood and owned.

Concrete defects the audit must confirm (all verified in the live code at HEAD, not hypothesized):

1. Two code paths with divergent conflict logic over the same rows. HouseholdSyncService.syncHousehold (data/services/HouseholdSyncService.ts:27) does pushLocalChanges() then pullRemoteChanges(), driven by an MMKV "household_last_sync_timestamp". HouseholdRealtimeService (data/services/HouseholdRealtimeService.ts) subscribes to Supabase postgres_changes on the stock table. The realtime path's handleUpdate DOES compare timestamps and skips stale writes (HouseholdRealtimeService.ts:142-150, `if (remoteUpdatedAt <= localUpdatedAt) return`); the batch pull path's prepareUpdate (HouseholdSyncService.ts:90-105) does NOT — it unconditionally overwrites every local field with the remote value. A user editing offline who then triggers a full sync can have their local edits silently clobbered.

2. Permanent deletes propagated as destructive operations. HouseholdRealtimeService.handleDelete calls `existing.destroyPermanently()` (HouseholdRealtimeService.ts:182) — not WatermelonDB's soft `markAsDeleted()`. There is no is_archived/deleted_at column on the stock table (confirmed in data/db/schema.ts:114-131). A fat-finger delete on device A irreversibly removes the row on device B with no undo and no tombstone, and the realtime handler only skips its own changes by added_by_user_id (HouseholdRealtimeService.ts:43-45), with no "this delete originated here" check.

3. No synced_at/sync_status on the synced model. ingredient_category (data/db/schema.ts:144) and the recipe table (data/db/schema.ts:31) carry synced_at columns; the stock table — the actual shared household surface — does not. Sync "progress" is only knowable via the global MMKV timestamp, so per-record sync state and retry-on-failure are impossible to reason about.

4. O(rows) full-table scans inside per-remote-item loops. pullRemoteChanges calls `stockCollection.query().fetch()` (all rows) then JS `.find()` on every remote item (HouseholdSyncService.ts:87-88); the same pattern recurs in handleInsert/handleUpdate/handleDelete (HouseholdRealtimeService.ts:92-93, 134-135, 176-177). On a shared pantry with hundreds of items this is an O(rows x remote-items) scan on the JS thread, re-run on every realtime event.

5. Errors swallowed and sync marker advanced on partial failure. HouseholdSyncService.syncHousehold wraps push+pull in one try/catch that only logs (HouseholdSyncService.ts:32-34); realtime handler failures are caught and logged per-event (e.g. HouseholdRealtimeService.ts:168-170) and never retried. There is no telemetry/Sentry breadcrumb distinguishing "sync succeeded with N conflicts" from "sync threw, data may diverge."

6. Sync runs only on explicit lifecycle actions, never on app foreground/reconnect. subscribe/syncHousehold/unsubscribe are invoked only from data/api/householdApi.ts (lines 37, 142-143, 209-210, 223, 271, 347) around create-household / join / leave / member-add flows. app/_layout.tsx has no household sync call (only an unrelated subscription-query import). Between those explicit actions, a device can show indefinitely stale data — itself a finding worth documenting.

Role clarity note (verified, not a defect): data/api/householdApi.ts is NOT a duplicate of data/supabase-api/HouseholdApi.ts. The former is the higher-level lifecycle orchestrator (imports the latter at line 1 and wraps its raw Supabase calls); the latter is the low-level Supabase API surface also imported directly by HouseholdSyncService.ts:2. The audit should document this layering explicitly so future readers do not mistake one for a stale copy of the other.

Per [F11], pure decentralized CRDTs cost measurable perf/memory and major engineering versus single-authority systems, and production leaders (Figma, Linear) ship HYBRID local-first-UX + server-authoritative sync. The expected outcome of this audit is confirmation that Cookkit IS already the pragmatic hybrid (Supabase is authoritative for shared rows; WatermelonDB is the offline cache) — NOT a recommendation to adopt CRDTs. This is the cheapest possible de-risking of P3-5 and a prerequisite for any correctness test suite.

DIRECTIONAL (not cited): recipe-manager/meal-planner competitors (Paprika, Mela, Plan to Eat, Mealime, Prepear) did not yield confirmed 3-vote sync-conflict claims in research, so we cannot benchmark our conflict UX against them. The audit should therefore define Cookkit's own correctness bar rather than chase parity.

Note on monetization risk: this is a research/audit issue, not an experiment, so the [F5]/[F9] refund-and-churn figures are category-agnostic medians for monetization A/B tests and do not directly apply here. However, sync data loss on a Pro feature is itself a churn/refund driver (directionally consistent with [F9]'s observation that AI-tier apps churn faster and refund more), which is the underlying motivation for documenting sync correctness before scaling households.

### Motivation
SuperCook's standout UX is one-tap, voice-dictation batch pantry entry — "open fridge, tap mic, speak ingredients" — which lets a user populate an entire pantry in seconds rather than typing or photographing items one at a time [F1]. This is directly copyable and Cookkit already owns most of the plumbing: hooks/useSpeechRecognition.ts is a production-hardened expo-speech-recognition wrapper (CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD=0.5 gating, AppState pause/resume, debounce, permission flow) that today is wired only for cooking commands. A 10-second path to a populated pantry (vs. today's camera-scan flow in app/ingredient/(create)/create.tsx, which routes through a Skia-based segmentation + classify pipeline in hooks/model/ that processes one photo at a time) is the fastest way to reach the personalized recommendation engine in hooks/recommendation/, which depends on a non-empty pantry. A voice path gives users a high-confidence fallback when the camera mis-segments or mis-classifies an item, and the same correction sheet (app/ingredient/(create)/confirmation.tsx) serves both inputs.\n\nNote on recipe-manager parity: claims that Paprika/Mela/Plan to Eat etc. lack voice pantry entry are DIRECTIONAL (not cited) — the dedicated recipe-manager segment did not yield confirmed research claims, so competitive parity arguments here are hypotheses, not evidence.

### Technical approach
1. ## P3-5 — Household multi-device sync hardening (conflict UX) based on the P0-3 audit

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dimension** | Performance |
| **Phase** | 3 — Differentiation & Performance |
| **Priority** | P3 |
| **Type** | feature |
| **Estimate** | L |
| **Depends on** | `P0-3` |

> **Summary:** Replace the naive last-write-wins push/pull in HouseholdSyncService.ts with a conflict-aware sync that detects concurrent edits, preserves them for undo, and surfaces resolution to the user — without over-investing in CRDTs. Scope and exact resolution policy are driven by the P0-3 audit findings.

### Motivation
Shared households (data/db/models/Household.ts, HouseholdMember.ts) mean two members can edit the same Stock item concurrently on separate devices. Today's HouseholdSyncService.syncHousehold() is a blind push-then-pull: pullRemoteChanges() fetches all local stock via stockCollection.query().fetch() (full-table scan, no household_id/updated_at filter in the WatermelonDB Q query) and unconditionally overwrites every local field with the remote payload inside existing.prepareUpdate(...). There is no comparison of item.updatedAt vs the incoming record's updated_at, no conflict log, no undo, and no user prompt. The result is silent data loss whenever two members edit the same item between syncs — e.g. one bumps quantity to 4, the other sets it to 2, and whoever pulls last silently wins with no trace.\n\nPer [F11], production leaders (Figma, Linear) deliberately avoid pure decentralized CRDTs because the cost is "measurable perf/memory + major engineering," and use a HYBRID local-first-UX + server-authoritative sync instead. Cookkit's WatermelonDB + Supabase stack is already that pragmatic hybrid, so this issue explicitly does NOT pursue CRDTs — it keeps Supabase as the authority and adds conflict detection + graceful resolution on top. The existing stock.updated_at column (number epoch, currently NOT indexed) and stock.household_id (already isIndexed: true in schema.ts) are the right foundation; this work makes them load-bearing for conflict detection rather than just bookkeeping. NOTE: a synced_at column exists on recipe and pantry_item tables in schema.ts but NOT on stock, so it must not be assumed present for this work.\n\nThis is also a retention/anti-churn investment for a monetized surface (Cookkit Pro households). [F9] shows AI/gadget apps monetize harder but churn ~30% faster with +20% refunds; silent data loss in a shared pantry is exactly the kind of "feature broke my data" incident that drives those refund spikes.
